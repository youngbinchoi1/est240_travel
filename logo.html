Identity Design

Rationale behind the mark — how palette, form, and scale choices encode the group's analytical framework and shared visual identity.

Large
Medium
Small
1 Globe + Pin — Denotation

The primary mark combines a wireframe globe with a map pin. At the denotative level, this communicates the group's subject matter directly: international travel, geography, and place. The wireframe globe references cartographic tradition and signals that the group's analytical framework is global in scope.

2 Amber on Black — Connotation

The amber-on-black palette connotes warmth, distance, and the quality of golden-hour light that defines travel photography. Against a near-black field, the amber reads as luminous and isolated — a single point of light on a dark map, evoking both adventure and the analogue aesthetics of vintage cartography.

3 Wireframe Reduction — Scalability

The globe is rendered as a minimal wireframe rather than a filled form. This choice ensures legibility at favicon scale, where complex illustrations collapse. The reduction to meridian lines and a single pin creates a mark that works across favicon, nav badge, and full-bleed header without loss of meaning.

4 Shared Identity with Personal Marks

The group logo is designed to function as a family with each member's personal mark. The shared amber accent and dark background establish visual continuity across pages, while the distinct forms — globe for the group, individual typographic marks for members — encode the collective-vs.-individual dynamic at the heart of the group project.
